How to Clean a Plumbing Snake: A Coppell, TX Guide

A plumbing snake, also called a drain auger, pulls out clogs on a coiled cable. After a job, the cable is coated in grease, hair and bacteria. Cleaning it right away keeps it from rusting and keeps the mess out of your house. This Coppell, TX homeowner guide gives the steps and the safety points.

Cleaning is easy, but it matters. A dirty snake stored wet will rust in a few weeks.

Before you start: protect yourself

Wear rubber gloves and eye protection. Drain waste carries bacteria. Work outside or in a tub or utility sink that is easy to disinfect. Put down a plastic sheet if you are working in a garage.

Do not use a snake if you suspect the line is a main sewer that is full of water and backing up. That needs a machine and a plumber, and it can splash.

Step by step cleaning

  1. Wipe off the heavy debris with a paper towel as you pull the cable back out of the drain.
  2. Rinse the cable with a garden hose, running water over each section as you pull it out.
  3. Scrub the cable with a stiff brush and dish soap.
  4. Spray or soak it with a household disinfectant and let it sit for several minutes.
  5. Rinse again until no soap or residue is left.
  6. Dry the cable fully with a towel.
  7. Wipe a light coat of machine oil on the cable.
  8. Store it in a dry place, coiled loosely in its drum.

What to avoid

Do not use strong acids on a cable. They eat at the metal. Do not store the cable wet in a closed drum. Moisture trapped inside turns to rust and spreads. Do not pour the rinse water into a drain that is already slow, since it may carry debris back into the line.

  • Do not mix bleach with ammonia or other cleaners.
  • Do not leave the cable coiled in a damp basement.
  • Do not force a kinked cable, since it can snap.

Inspecting the cable and tip

After cleaning, check the cable for kinks, flattened spots and rust. A kinked cable can break inside a drain. Check the tip, the auger head, for sharp edges and wear. A worn tip does not catch debris well. Replace it or the whole snake if the metal is damaged.

Types of snakes and care differences

A hand crank snake is the simplest to clean. A drum auger has a cable inside a drum and needs more drying time. A powered machine used by plumbers needs more care and a full disassembly of some parts.

When a clog is beyond a homeowner snake

A snake works for a sink, a tub or a toilet. It does not clean pipe walls and it will not fix a collapsed line. If the clog returns, call a plumber. Drain cleaning and sewer inspection cover the next steps.

How to tell your snake is worn out

A cable can wear in ways that are not obvious. Look for flat spots where the coil has kinked, bright metal where the surface has rubbed away and rust that does not clean off. A snake with a kink can break when it turns inside a pipe, and a broken cable end is hard to retrieve.

If you use the snake rarely, replace it when it starts to show rust. If you use it often, expect a few years of service. Keep the manual, because some models have a specific way to wind the cable back into the drum.

Safer disposal of what comes out of a drain

What you pull out of a drain is a mix of hair, grease and waste. Put it in a bag and in the trash, not back down another drain. Wash your hands and any tools that touched it. Wipe the area with a disinfectant, and let it dry before you put things back.

If you used a snake in a toilet or a floor drain, wear gloves and wash up carefully afterward. Children and pets should stay out of the area until it is clean.

Storing the snake so it is ready next time

Choose a dry spot off the floor, such as a garage shelf. Keep the drum or cable away from chemicals and from water heaters that could drip. Wrap the cable with a clean rag in a light film of oil if you will not use it for months. Label the drum with the date you last cleaned it.

Keep a pair of gloves and a small bottle of disinfectant with the snake. When the next clog comes, you will be ready, and you will be more likely to clean the tool afterward instead of leaving it dirty.
