Sewage backup: what to do first

A stopped main line sends wastewater to the lowest opening in the house, often a shower drain or a basement-level floor drain. Stop using sinks, tubs, dishwashers and washers right away. Flushing makes it worse.

Keep family and pets away from the wet area and open windows if there is an odor. Take photos for your insurer. Tell the dispatcher where the cleanout is, because it can save a full hour of work.

What a main line cleaning involves

We find the cleanout, usually a capped pipe near the house, and open it. A machine with a cutting head runs through the line, breaking up roots, grease and packed debris. For heavier buildup, hydro jetting washes the pipe wall clean.

When the water drains freely, we run water to prove the line is clear. If the clog came back from the same spot before, we put a camera in after the cleaning so you see the cause instead of guessing.

Roots, clay soil and older lines

The Dallas County Master Gardeners describe clay soil in this region as swelling when wet and shrinking when dry. That movement opens small gaps in old joints, and roots follow the water. Large trees near the line are the usual suspects, but even small shrubs can send roots in.

Cutting roots buys time but does not close the gap. If roots return every year, a repair or liner is the real answer, and we will say so.

Apartment, rental and small commercial lines

Landlords and property managers call about backups that affect several units. A main line that serves a building is larger and carries more waste, so we plan for a heavier machine and a camera. See commercial plumbing if the property is a business or a multi-unit building.

Tell us whether tenants have reported the problem and how many units are affected. That helps us prioritize and bring the right equipment.
