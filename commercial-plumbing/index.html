What to do in an after-hours plumbing emergency at a business

Close the main shutoff to the affected area and, for backups, stop all water use in the building. Warn staff about slippery floors and block access to wet electrical areas. If a restaurant kitchen is affected, tell the dispatcher what is backing up, because grease lines need different equipment.

Take photos for your insurer and your landlord. Note the time you first noticed the problem and what you did, since that timeline matters for both.

Plumbing needs by property type

Restaurants deal with grease, floor drains and heavy hot water demand. Offices and retail spaces deal with restroom fixtures, break room sinks and water heaters. Multi-unit buildings have shared stacks and mains that serve many tenants, so a stoppage can reach several units.

Coppell has a large business base near the DFW Airport corridor and along Sandy Lake Road and Denton Tap Road, and Cypress Waters adds newer office space. We bring the right equipment for the pipe size and the building.

Property managers and landlords

If you manage several units, tell us whether the work order comes from a tenant and who approves cost. We document findings in plain language so you can pass them to owners. We also note what falls on the tenant and what falls on the building.

For recurring problems, a camera inspection and a planned cleaning schedule are usually cheaper than repeated emergency calls.

Grease, backflow and compliance

Restaurants may have a grease trap or interceptor that must be cleaned on a schedule. Backflow devices may need testing under local rules. Ask Coppell's city offices about what applies to your property, and tell us if you need help with a repair on a trap or device that has failed.

We do not claim to handle work that needs a separate licensed specialty. We say so plainly and help you find the right trade.
