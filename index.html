What to do in a plumbing emergency in Coppell

Start with the water. If a pipe has burst or a fixture is flooding, close the main shutoff valve, usually on an exterior wall near the front of the house or in the garage. Then call our 24/7 line. We answer nights, weekends and holidays, and a dispatcher asks where the water is, how fast it is moving and whether anyone is at risk.

If a drain is backing up, stop running water in the house. If a heater tank is leaking, shut off its cold water supply and cut the power or gas. If you smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 before you call a plumber. We tell you plainly what we see and give you a price before work starts.

What Coppell homes ask of their plumbing

Coppell grew quickly after DFW Airport opened in 1974, and most of its residential land was built out by about 2000. That puts many homes at 25 to 50 years old, an age when water heaters have been replaced once or twice, supply lines show wear and buried sewer pipe may have joints that have shifted.

The soil matters too. The Dallas County Master Gardeners describe the region's clay soil as swelling when wet and shrinking when dry. That movement can stress pipe in and under slabs and buried lines. It is one reason slab leaks and sewer problems show up in North Texas.

Water in Coppell and what it means at your tap

Coppell buys its drinking water from Dallas Water Utilities. According to the city's 2025 water quality report, the average total chlorine residual is 3.10 parts per million, and the report does not publish a hardness figure. We do not guess one. A home test strip tells you how hard your water is, and that decides how often to flush a heater or whether a softener is worth it.

Coppell has also been through hard weeks for its water and power. The June 2023 loss of a Village Parkway pump station put the city into Stage 5 water restrictions, and Stage 2 restrictions followed in August 2023. In February 2021 a deep freeze left about 11,500 Coppell residents without power on the Monday of the storm. Knowing where your main valve is, and how to protect exposed pipe, pays off in weeks like those.
