What Old Town Coppell is, and what it is not

According to the Coppell Historical Society, Old Town sits along Bethel and South Coppell Roads on the site of the former Gibbs settlement, where a post office opened in 1887 in the Harrison family general store. The community took the Coppell name in 1892 after the Cotton Belt railroad arrived.

The city set up the modern Old Town area through its 1996 comprehensive plan, and a 2002 Old Coppell Master Plan followed. Community Impact reported in 2023 that about 70 acres have been developed or redeveloped, and that a 2014 change allowed residential use. The district is a planning area with a mix of uses, not a single subdivision with fixed boundaries, so we describe it by its anchor roads instead of drawing a border.

Homes, shops and restaurants on the same blocks

Real-estate listing data labels streets such as South Coppell Road, Hammond Street and Cameron Court as Old Town or Old Town Main Street addresses. The newer Main Street Old Town project, according to Community Impact, includes office, residential and retail space, and the city approved a mixed-use property at 509 W. Bethel Road in 2023.

For a plumbing call, the first useful question is what the building is. A house, a townhome-style unit, a restaurant and a small office all have different fixtures, different drains and different people responsible for the pipes.

Older buildings and new construction side by side

Old Town includes buildings from different eras next to recent infill. That is the reason not to guess. Do not assume an older building has old pipe, and do not assume a newer one is trouble free. A camera look at the drain line and a visual check of supply piping tells you what is actually there.

The city's zoning code has a historic district chapter. If your property is a historic landmark or sits in an overlay, ask the city whether any review applies before you start work that changes the building.

Permits and licensing before you hire

Coppell Building Inspections lists a water heater permit type on its permits page and asks homeowners to call 972-304-3500 for plumbing requirements. The office is at 265 East Parkway Boulevard and answers weekdays from 8 am to 5 pm.

Texas generally requires plumbing work to be done by licensed plumbers, and the Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners lets you look up a license number on its website. Ask for that number and check it before you agree to a job.

Before you call about an Old Town problem

A few notes make the first conversation shorter.

Find the shutoff

Photograph the problem

A photo of the stain, the leak or the fixture helps a plumber guess which parts to bring.
