Start with the water you actually have

Coppell buys treated water from Dallas Water Utilities. The city's 2025 report lists an average total chlorine residual of 3.10 parts per million, and it does not give a hardness number. Many homes notice a chlorine taste or smell, and some see scale on fixtures. Hardness varies by the source mix, so test the tap you use.

A test strip kit gives a quick hardness reading, and a lab test gives more detail. Ask the utility for its current report. We use your numbers to decide if a softener, a carbon filter or neither makes sense.

Signs your water may need treatment

  • ✓White crust on faucets, showerheads and glass.
  • ✓Soap that does not lather well.
  • ✓Chlorine taste or smell in drinking water.
  • ✓Spots on dishes after the dishwasher.
  • ✓Scale buildup in the water heater, see water heater repair.

What installation involves

We pick a spot near the main, usually in the garage or a utility closet, and add a bypass valve so you can isolate the unit. The system needs a drain for regeneration and a power outlet for a softener. We test pressure and flow after installation and walk you through the settings.

Softeners and filters add resistance, so we check that your pressure stays good at the far fixtures. If it does not, we advise on a larger unit or a different position.

Protecting appliances and fixtures

Scale shortens the life of water heaters, dishwashers and faucets. A tankless unit is especially sensitive, so see tankless water heater service for flushing. If you already own a softener that is not working, we can look at the valve, the brine tank and the resin.

Be honest about maintenance. Softeners need salt, and filters need new cartridges. If you do not want that routine, a smaller solution may fit better.

Choosing between a softener, a filter and doing nothing

Not every home needs treatment. If a test shows moderate hardness and you see no scale, you may be fine. If you see scale on fixtures, spots on glass and buildup in the heater, a softener starts to make sense. If your complaint is chlorine taste, a carbon filter solves it without the salt routine.

We would rather you buy the smaller solution that fits than a large system you will not maintain. A short test and a conversation about what you notice at the tap usually points to the right answer.

Questions to ask before you buy any system

  • ✓What does the water test say, and who ran it?
  • ✓How much salt or how many cartridges does it use each year?
  • ✓Does it need a drain and a power outlet?
  • ✓What happens when it is time to service it?
  • ✓How does it affect flow at the far fixtures?
