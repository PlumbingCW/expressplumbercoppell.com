Active leak: what to do before we arrive

If water is coming through a ceiling, running down a wall or pooling on the floor, close the main shutoff valve first. In many Coppell homes it sits on an exterior wall near the front, or in the garage where the supply enters the house. Turn it clockwise until it stops.

Then switch off power to the affected room at the breaker if water is near outlets or light fixtures, move valuables, and put a bucket under any ceiling bulge. Do not poke a hole in a bulging ceiling near light fixtures. Call us and describe where the water is and how fast it is coming.

Meter still running with everything off

The fastest do-it-yourself test uses your water meter. Turn off every tap and appliance, then watch the meter. Coppell explains that the right-most digit on the meter is the leak indicator, and if it keeps moving while nothing runs, water is leaking somewhere between the meter and the house or inside it.

Next, shut the main valve at the house and look again. If the meter stops, the leak is inside the home. If it keeps moving with the valve closed, the loss is on the buried line between the meter and the house, which is the homeowner side of the connection. Tell us which result you saw and the call goes faster.

A high water bill and what it can mean

A bill that jumps with no change in habits is one of the most common reasons people call. Toilet flappers that leak into the bowl, irrigation zones stuck on, a leaking water heater relief valve and a slow slab leak all look the same on paper, which is a larger number than last month.

Coppell lists a leak repair affidavit that can be used once every 24 months, and the number given for it is 972-304-3695. Terms change, so confirm what the city requires, and keep the plumber invoice and the repair date, since those are usually the paperwork behind any adjustment request.

Buried water service line leaks

The line between the meter and the house often runs through clay soil. The Dallas County Master Gardeners note that clay soil in this region swells when wet and shrinks when dry, and that repeated movement is hard on anything buried in it. Over many seasons, a buried supply line can crack, pull at a fitting or rub through where it crosses another pipe.

Signs include a meter that runs with the house shut off, an unexplained wet patch, a section of lawn that stays green in a dry week, and a drop in pressure. Repair options range from a spot repair after we locate the break to replacing the run. We explain the choices and price before digging.

Leaks above ceilings and inside walls

A brown ring on a ceiling can come from a leaking supply line, a drain line, an air conditioner condensate pan or the roof. We check the plumbing first because that is what we can prove with a moisture map and a pressure test.

If the stain is under a bathroom, the shower pan, the tub drain and the toilet flange are the usual suspects. If it is under an attic air handler, the condensate line is more likely. In either case, do not wait. Wet drywall can grow mold within days and sag under the weight of trapped water. If you also have a slow or backing-up drain, mention it, because the fix may sit in drain cleaning rather than a supply repair.

Irrigation and outdoor leak sources

Sprinkler systems add many joints and valves to a yard, and a stuck valve can pour water for days before anyone notices. If your meter runs only when the irrigation clock is idle, check each zone valve for a weeping diaphragm and every head for a cracked riser.

If the leak is on the house side of the sprinkler backflow device, we can usually repair it. Systems that need a licensed irrigator are separate, and we tell you plainly when that applies.
