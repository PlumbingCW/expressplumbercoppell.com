What a proper install includes

A heater is more than a box on a pad. A good installation sets the tank level, adds a drain pan where leaks could damage finished space, and runs the relief valve discharge to a safe spot. Dielectric unions or flexible connectors keep dissimilar metals apart.

On gas units we connect the line with a sediment trap, test every joint, and verify the vent pulls the exhaust out. On electric units we confirm the circuit, wire size and breaker match the heater label.

Details that separate a clean install from a callback

  • ✓Thermal expansion tank where the home has a closed system or pressure regulator.
  • ✓Pan and drain line where the heater sits above finished space.
  • ✓Temperature set at the level you want, commonly 120 degrees Fahrenheit.
  • ✓Shutoff valves labeled and accessible for the next owner.
  • ✓Relief valve piped within code to a visible, safe discharge.

Why an expansion tank matters

When water heats up, it expands. In a closed plumbing loop, that extra volume has nowhere to go, and pressure climbs. A small expansion tank absorbs it. Without one, repeated pressure spikes can stress the heater, the relief valve and fixture connections.

Homes with a pressure reducing valve or a backflow device on the supply are normally closed systems. We test the static pressure at your hose bib and recommend a tank only when the numbers support it.

Permits and licensed work in Coppell

Permit rules vary, so ask the Coppell building department whether a new water heater needs one. We can walk you through the usual answer and what an inspector looks for, including relief piping and venting.

The Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners recommends confirming the Responsible Master Plumber license number before you hire, and its public search shows whether insurance is on file. That is a good habit for every contractor you hire for home work.

Installing alongside other plumbing work

Heater installs are a good time to fix old shutoff valves, replace aging supply lines and add a softener loop if you plan one. See plumbing installation for related fixture and line work done in the same visit.

If your supply piping is galvanized or very old, ask about repiping before the new heater is connected to it. A new tank on tired pipe is the weak link you want to avoid.
