When a backup calls for jetting right away

If sewage is backing up into a tub or floor drain and a cable only opened a small channel, the pipe is probably packed with grease or roots. Jetting clears that mass and flushes it down the line instead of tearing a hole in it.

While you wait, do not run water, do not use the toilet unless you must, and keep children and pets out of the wet area. Mention the cleanout location when you call, since it speeds up the work.

How jetting works

A flexible hose with a nozzle carries water at high pressure. Jets pointed backward pull the hose forward, and jets pointed forward and outward cut into buildup and rinse it away. The water pressure used on household lines is much lower than what industrial crews use on large sewers, and we choose the setting for your pipe size and material.

On older cast iron pipe, jetting can also remove tuberculation, which is rough rust scale inside the pipe that catches debris. That leaves a smoother bore and slows the next clog.

Roots and the clay soil factor

Clay soil across the Dallas and Fort Worth region swells and shrinks with moisture, as the Dallas County Master Gardeners describe. Over years that movement can open small gaps at buried pipe joints, and tree roots find those gaps and grow inside the pipe.

Jetting cuts and flushes the roots but does not repair the gap. If the camera shows roots at a joint, we explain the options, which range from regular maintenance jetting to a section repair under sewer line repair.

Commercial and restaurant lines

Restaurants and apartment buildings in the Coppell area produce heavy grease loads. Jetting is common for kitchen drains and trap lines. If your business is on a grease program, see commercial plumbing for scheduled work.
