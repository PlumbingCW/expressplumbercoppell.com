What we install

Sinks, faucets, toilets, tubs, showers, utility sinks, washing machine boxes, ice maker lines and outdoor hose bibs are all routine. We also install pressure reducing valves, expansion tanks and whole-home shutoffs. For heaters, see water heater installation.

Each job gets the right shutoff valves, new supply lines and a leak test before we leave. Old parts that fail often, such as plastic supply lines and fixture shutoffs that never close, get replaced rather than reused.

Good times to add or update plumbing

  • ✓Before a kitchen or bath remodel closes up walls.
  • ✓When you replace a vanity or a tub and the shutoffs are old.
  • ✓When you add a bathroom, laundry or wet bar.
  • ✓When you buy a house with hard-to-turn valves.
  • ✓When a pressure gauge shows high static pressure.

Permits and code in Coppell

Permit requirements depend on the scope of work, so ask the Coppell building department before you start. We install to the plumbing code the city has adopted, and we can tell you in advance whether the job is likely to need an inspection.

You can confirm any contractor with the Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners public license search. The Responsible Master Plumber number on an estimate is the key to look up.

Plumbing in Coppell's older and newer homes

Much of Coppell was built after DFW Airport opened in 1974 and was fully built out by about 2000. That means many homes have original supply lines and valves that are due for replacement. If you find several worn parts at once, ask about repiping.

Newer neighborhoods such as Cypress Waters have more recent plumbing, so the work there is usually fixture and appliance upgrades. Either way, we check the water pressure first, because high pressure shortens the life of every fixture.

Water treatment and fixtures that go together

Adding a softener or filter often involves new shutoffs and a bypass valve. See water softener and filtration for what that work includes.

Preparing for an installation visit

A little preparation shortens the visit. Clear the area under the sink or around the fixture. Turn on the water at the main valve in the morning so we can test the shutoffs. Have the fixture and its parts unboxed and the instructions handy. If you have the receipt and warranty card, keep them with you.

Tell us about anything unusual, such as a past leak, a remodel that moved plumbing or a fixture that never quite worked. That history saves time because we look in the right place first.

Things that make an installation go smoothly

  • ✓Know where the main valve is and that it turns.
  • ✓Confirm the fixture fits the space and the hole count.
  • ✓Ask whether old parts are being replaced or reused.
  • ✓Ask about any permit or inspection for the work.
  • ✓Plan for a short water shutoff during the work.
