What we found about Waterside Estates

Listing records label the subdivision Waterside Estates with Coppell addresses in ZIP 75019. A real-estate guide reports observed build years of 1992 to 1997 in recent sales, with one-story and two-story homes from about 2,467 to 4,037 square feet and a median near 3,300 square feet.

The same guide says some homes overlook canals, a few allow kayak launching from the backyard and several have pools. That is the heart of the neighborhood's plumbing angle. Water use outside the house can be big, and it can hide a leak.

High water bill or leak? A simple meter test

A pool, a landscape and an irrigation system can raise a bill in the hot months with no plumbing fault at all. A real leak behaves differently. It keeps running when nothing is using water.

Turn off every tap and appliance, then look at the water meter. If the small flow indicator is moving, water is going somewhere. If it holds still, the high use is likely coming from something you are running on purpose.

  • ✓Include the irrigation controller, the pool auto-fill and the ice maker in your check.
  • ✓Repeat the test at night when nobody is home.
  • ✓Photograph the meter so you can compare readings an hour apart.

Water near the house

Per the real-estate guide, some lots sit at the water's edge. We have no data on any specific lot's drainage, and we do not claim the area has more leaks than anywhere else. A few habits are sensible wherever the yard meets water.

  • ✓Keep downspouts and drain lines pointed away from the foundation.
  • ✓Look at outdoor spigots for drips after you turn them off.
  • ✓Check that the irrigation system is not watering the foundation.

Billing, permits and the association

Coppell's sewer billing used a winter averaging method, as reported by Community Impact in 2022. The city looks at November through March water use. If you fill a pool or run heavy use in those months, ask city utility billing how it counts.

According to the permits page, water heaters have a permit type, and Building Inspections can be reached at 972-304-3500. A real-estate guide lists historical association dues, which change, so confirm current rules with the association.

What to do if you suspect a leak

Take these steps in order and keep notes.

Find the main valve

Know where to close the main water valve before you need it.

Run the meter test

Check the meter with everything off, then again an hour later.

Look outside first

Check hose bibs, irrigation and the pool fill line for steady water.

Document it
