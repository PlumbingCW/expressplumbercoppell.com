How a tankless heater differs from a tank

A tank heater keeps 40 to 50 gallons hot all day. A tankless unit fires a burner or elements only when a tap opens, so the heat exchanger carries the load. Hot water is steady, but only up to the flow rate the unit can heat.

That makes sizing important. If two showers and a dishwasher run at once, a unit that is too small gives lukewarm water. We size by the number of fixtures and your incoming water temperature, not just by the size of the house.

When a tankless unit needs service

  • ✓It shows an error code and shuts off.
  • ✓Hot water turns cold in short bursts.
  • ✓You hear boiling or kettle noises in the unit.
  • ✓It has not been flushed in more than a year.
  • ✓The flow is weaker than before.

Flush and descale: the maintenance that matters

Minerals in the water coat the inside of the heat exchanger as it works. That scale reduces heat transfer and can trigger overheating shutdowns. A flush circulates a descaling solution through the unit, usually with a small pump and hoses connected to the service valves.

Units that have service valves installed flush easily. Units without them need valves added first. We check the inlet screen, run the flush and test the unit afterward. Coppell's water report does not list hardness, so a test strip on your tap helps set the flush schedule.

Switching from a tank to tankless

A swap involves more than a heater change. The vent usually changes to a sealed stainless or plastic pipe through the wall or roof. The gas line may need a larger size, and the unit needs a power outlet, a condensate drain for high efficiency models and sometimes a pressure relief setup.

We check those items before quoting so you do not hit surprise add-ons. If your current tank is failing, compare the full cost with a standard tank replacement and decide on the home you have, not on a brochure.
