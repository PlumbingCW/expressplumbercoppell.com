Signs of a slab leak you should not ignore

A hissing or running sound with no fixture on, a warm patch on a tile floor, damp carpet by an exterior wall, or a meter that keeps moving with the house shut off are the common signs. Water can also rise along a foundation edge or show up as a crack with moisture.

Close the main valve and call. A slab leak left alone keeps feeding water under the foundation, raises your bill and can soften the soil under a corner of the slab.

Why slabs leak in North Texas

The soil under Coppell homes is expansive clay, part of the Blackland Prairie belt. Clay holds water and swells, then shrinks in drought, and the movement is hard on pipe that was poured into or under concrete. Most Coppell homes are 25 to 50 years old, built after DFW Airport opened in 1974, so the pipes under many slabs are old enough to fail.

Copper lines can pit from the outside where they touch rough concrete, and a hot line can fail sooner because heat adds expansion stress. Water chemistry matters too, and Coppell's utility report does not list hardness, so we do not blame any single cause until we see the pipe.

Damage to floors and the water bill

Water under a slab wicks into flooring and baseboards, and it can raise indoor humidity. Hardwood and laminate cup and lift, carpet gets musty and tile can loosen from the mortar bed. Document everything with dated photos before anything is removed.

Run the meter test again after repair to confirm the line holds. Coppell's leak repair affidavit for water bills is listed at 972-304-3695, so ask about it and keep the plumbing invoice and repair date.

Insurance and slab leaks

Coverage differs a lot between policies. Some pay for access and repair of the damage caused by a sudden leak, some exclude slab leaks, and almost all exclude simple wear. A claim often needs a plumber's written report showing the cause and the location.

We write a plain description of what we found. We never promise a claim outcome. Call your insurer before approving repairs, and keep the damaged materials until an adjuster has looked.
