Disposal leaking or backing up: first steps

Turn off the disposal switch and, if you can reach it, the circuit breaker. Never put a hand into a disposal, even when it looks off. Close the angle stops under the sink if the leak is from a supply line.

If dirty water rises in the other basin, the drain line past the disposal is blocked. Stop running water and call. That is a drain cleaning job, not a disposal job, and both can be handled in one visit.

Why disposals jam, hum and leak

A humming disposal that will not spin usually has a jam from a bone, a fruit pit or a stuck utensil. Most units have a hex socket on the bottom for manual rotation, and a red reset button on the housing. A leak from the top is the sink flange seal, a leak from the side is a hose or gasket, and a leak from the bottom means the case itself has failed.

Grease and starchy foods such as rice and pasta swell and coat the drain line past the disposal. That is why many disposal problems show up as slow or backing-up sinks, not as a motor issue.

What not to put down a disposal

  • ✓Grease, oil and fat that harden in the pipe.
  • ✓Coffee grounds in large amounts.
  • ✓Rice, pasta and potato peels that swell.
  • ✓Bones, fruit pits and corn husks.
  • ✓Fibrous vegetables like celery and onion skins in bulk.
