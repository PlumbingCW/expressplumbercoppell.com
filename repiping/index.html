Repiping in Coppell, TX

Repiping in Coppell, TX replaces a home's supply lines when old pipe keeps leaking, water runs brown or pressure drops. We explain the pipe options, the order of work and the walls we need to open before you commit to a price.

Signs a home needs repiping

One leak does not mean the whole system is done. A pattern does. Repeated pinhole leaks, rust colored water from hot and cold taps, falling pressure and fittings that crumble when touched are the signs we look for.

Homes built in Coppell after 1974 and into the 1990s may have copper, CPVC or, in some regions, older materials such as polybutylene. We identify the pipe in your home before suggesting anything.

Pipe materials you may find in a Texas home

  • ✓Copper: long lived, but pinholes can form from the outside or inside.
  • ✓CPVC: common in some builds, becomes brittle with age.
  • ✓PEX: flexible plastic used in newer work and repipes.
  • ✓Galvanized steel: older homes, rusts from the inside.
  • ✓Polybutylene: gray plastic known for failing at fittings.

PEX or copper for your repipe

Both are accepted by code. The choice comes down to budget, access and preference.

How a repipe is done

We shut off the water, install new lines from the main to each fixture and cut small access openings in walls and ceilings. In many homes the new lines run through the attic and down, which avoids the slab. If you have a slab leak history, see slab leak repair for the reroute approach.

We patch openings with drywall ready for paint, test the system at pressure and flush the lines before turning the home back on. Most homes can be repiped in a few days, depending on size.

Water quality and Coppell supply

Coppell's drinking water comes from Dallas Water Utilities, and the city's 2025 water quality report listed an average total chlorine residual of 3.10 parts per million. Discolored water that shows up only at a few taps often points to old pipe inside the house, not the utility.

If your water looks the same at every tap, including after a fresh flush of the line, check with the utility. If it is rusty at the old galvanized runs only, that supports a repipe. Questions about filtration are covered in water softener and filtration.

Permits and licensed work

Ask the Coppell building department whether your repipe needs a permit and inspection. Repipes are significant work, and an inspection protects you at resale. You can also verify any plumber on the Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners public license search by the Responsible Master Plumber number.
